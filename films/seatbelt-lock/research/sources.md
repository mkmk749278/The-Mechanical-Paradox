## Standards and regulators
- **[F209] US FMVSS No. 209, 49 CFR 571.209** (eCFR, current text),
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-571/subpart-B/section-571.209
  - S3: "Emergency-locking retractor means a retractor incorporating adjustment hardware by means of a locking mechanism
    that is activated by vehicle acceleration, webbing movement relative to the vehicle, or other automatic action
    during an emergency and is capable when locked of withstanding restraint forces."
  - S3: "Load-limiter means a seat belt assembly component or feature that controls tension on the seat belt to
    modulate the forces that are imparted to occupants restrained by the belt assembly during a crash."
  - S4.3(j)(2)(i)(D): "For a retractor sensitive to vehicle acceleration, lock when tilted at any angle greater than 45
    degrees from the angle at which it is installed in the vehicle or meet the requirements of S4.3(j)(2)(ii)."
  - S4.3(j)(2)(i)(E): "For a retractor sensitive to vehicle acceleration, not lock when the retractor is rotated in any
    direction to any angle of 15 degrees or less from its orientation in the vehicle."
  - S4.3(j)(2)(ii): "Shall lock before the webbing payout exceeds the maximum limit of 25 mm when the retractor is
    subjected to an acceleration of 0.7 g … The retractor is determined to be locked when the webbing belt load
    tension is at least 35 N."
  - S4.3(j)(2)(iii): "For a retractor sensitive to webbing withdrawal, shall not lock before the webbing payout extends
    to the minimum limit of 51 mm when the retractor is subjected to an acceleration no greater than 0.3 g"
  - S4.3(j)(2)(i)(A): "Exert a retractive force of not less than 1 N and not more than 7 N when attached to a strap or
    webbing that restrains both the upper torso and the pelvis"
  - S5.2(j)(2)(ii) is titled "Gravitational locking".
- **[FR11] Federal Register 76 (167), 29 Aug 2011, FMVSS 208 lockability**,
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2011-08-29/html/2011-21946.htm
  - "An ELR/ALR retractor can be converted from an ELR to an ALR by slowly pulling all of the webbing out of the
    retractor and then letting the retractor wind the webbing back up. In the ALR mode, the seat belt is lockable for
    use with CRSs."
  - fn 3: "An ELR is a seat belt retractor that locks only in response to the rapid deceleration of the vehicle or rapid
    spooling out of the seat belt webbing from the retractor, and increases the comfort of the seat belt assembly as
    compared to an automatic locking retractor (ALR). An ALR is a seat belt retractor that locks when the continuous
    motion of spooling the belt out is stopped."
- **[N13] NHTSA DOT HS 811 835 (Nov 2013), Effectiveness of Pretensioners and Load Limiters for Enhancing Fatality
  Reduction by Seat Belts**, https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/Publication/811835
  - "Pretensioners retract the seat belt to remove excess slack almost instantly upon sensing the vehicle has crashed,
    typically by firing a pyrotechnic device."
  - "When forces on the shoulder belt rise above a predetermined level, load limiters allow the belt to give or yield
    while controlling the tension in the belt, typically by spooling it out of the retractor, to avoid concentrating
    too much force on the occupant's chest."
  - "Pretensioners are typically pyrotechnic devices but some are electro-mechanical. They are triggered by the same
    crash sensors that are used to determine the need to deploy the vehicle's air bags."
  - "The torsion bar is a metal rod that will twist when sufficient torque is applied. In minor collisions, the torsion
    bar will hold its shape, and the seat belt retractor will lock normally. But, when the force applied by the webbing
    reaches the design limit, the torsion bar twists and allows the webbing to spool out of the retractor."
  - "It might be surmised that load limiters would especially benefit older occupants (who are more vulnerable to high
    belt loads), but not tall or heavy occupants (who might spool out the belt too far and contact interior
    components); the analysis results lean slightly, but not yet convincingly, in those directions."
  - "a belted driver or right-front passenger has an estimated 12.8 percent lower fatality risk if the belt is equipped
    with a pretensioner and a load limiter than if it is not equipped with either (95% confidence bounds: 2.6% to
    23.0%)" (cars, CUVs and minivans).
  - "By model year 2008, all new cars and LTVs sold in the United States were equipped with pretensioners and load
    limiters at the driver's and right-front passenger's seats."
  - "Mercedes-Benz introduced pretensioners in the front seats of their S-class cars in 1981."
- **[N03] NHTSA DOT HS 809 562 (Mar 2003), NCAP Test Improvements with Pretensioners and Load Limiters**,
  https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/809562
  - "Energy management systems allow seat belts to yield in a crash, preventing the shoulder belt from directing too
    much energy on the chest of the occupant."
  - "Other examples of energy management systems that have been used include tear stitching, and sheet metal tearing
    or torsion rod twisting within the retractor."
- **[UN09] UN ECE/TRANS/WP.29/GRSP/2009/6 (CLEPA), Regulation No. 16**,
  https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/668233/files/%5BE_%5DECE_TRANS_WP.29_GRSP_2009_6-EN.pdf
  - "a test acceleration of 2.0 g, which is the upper limit of the available locking range for webbing sensitive
    locking (0.8 – 2.0 g see paragraphs 6.2.5.3.1.2. and 6.2.5.3.2.)"; "sensitivity for vehicle deceleration (upper
    limit 0.45 g)". A 2009 working paper quoting the regulation: background only; the film's numbers are FMVSS 209's.

## Patents (the mechanism)
- **[P86] US 4,485,986 (priority 1982-01-28), Emergency locking retractor for a seat belt assembly**,
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US4485986A/en
  - "the inertia member has a greater mass than the combined mass of the disc cam and the sensor arm, so that upon
    rapid rotation of the reel due to webbing extension in an emergency, the inertia member lags behind the cam disc
    and so acts on the first end of the sensor arm to cause same to pivot from the retracted to the working position."
  - "A vehicle sensor mechanism includes a pawl capable of engaging the ratchet wheel in the event of excessive vehicle
    acceleration."
  - "Other adoptable types include those having a weight suspended in a pendulum fashion, and a ball rollable on a
    conical surface."
  - "A reel for paying off and winding up the webbing is fixedly mounted on a rotary shaft sprung in a direction of
    webbing retraction."
- **[P98] US 6,158,685 (priority 1998-07-13), Blocking mechanism for a seat belt retractor**,
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US6158685A/en
  - "an inertial disc (20) used as a belt webbing-sensitive sensor element … the actuating element (26a) causes the
    ratchet pawl (24) to engage with the ratchet toothing (16) when the belt reel (10) is rotated relative to the
    inertial disc (20)."

## Crash research (the catch)
- **[FB01] Foret-Bruno J-Y et al., "Comparison of thoracic injury risk in frontal car crashes for occupant restrained
  without belt load limiters and those restrained with 6 kN and 4 kN belt load limiters", Stapp Car Crash Journal 45
  (2001): 205–224, SAE 2001-22-0009** (abstract via https://wbldb.lievers.net/10017931.html; PubMed 17458746)
  - "the oldest occupants very often sustain severe thoracic injuries due to the conventional seat belt"
  - "the belt load limiter was a steel component designed to shear at a given shoulder force, namely 6 kN"
  - "The threshold was decreased to 4 kN and this lower belt belt-force limiter has been combined with a specially
    designed airbag."
  - "This paper reports on 347 real-world frontal accidents where the EES (Equivalent Energy Speed) ranged from 35 to
    75 km/h."
  - "50 to 60% reduction for AIS 2+ was observed, as well as 75 to 85% for AIS 3+."
  - "The complete absence of AIS 4+ with a 4 kN load limiter must be stressed, though it remains more than 8% for the
    other samples (no limiter and 6 kN limiter)."
