## For step 2 (research must confirm before the treatment)
1. The emergency-locking retractor: its two triggers (vehicle-sensitive mass or pendulum; webbing-sensitive
   flywheel or inertia disc) and how each throws the pawl into the toothed ring. Patents and standard text.
2. The thresholds each standard sets: the vehicle deceleration at which it must lock, the webbing acceleration, the
   tilt angle; and the speeds or tilts at which it must not lock (FMVSS 209 S4.3(j), ECE R16: cite the clauses).
3. Why it locks on a steep slope and when buckling a child seat (the automatic-locking mode: which cars, which rule).
4. The catch: the load limiter (torsion bar or tear webbing) that lets the belt pay out above a set force, its force
   range and why (chest injury), and the pretensioner (pyrotechnic, fired by the airbag unit: the one electronic part,
   said honestly).
5. Who invented the retractor lock and when, only if it is a story that raises the stakes (§7.9.5).
6. Real footage: a belt locking, a retractor cutaway (commercial licence, §8.8).
