# Film 3: the seatbelt that knows, treatment

**Logline.** Your seatbelt can tell you reaching for your phone from you hitting a wall, and the lock that decides
needs no chip: two small weights do it, one that feels the belt and one that feels the car. But a belt that stops
dead puts the whole crash through a strap across your chest, so the real one is built to lock and then, in a hard
crash, to let you go.

**The broken intuition (0:00–0:10).** "A crash sensor, wired to the car's computer, like the airbag." Most viewers
hold this model, and it is half right: there *is* such a sensor in the car, and it fires the pretensioner (C10). But
the lock itself needs none. The film breaks the model on the viewer's own experience: slow pull free, sharp pull dead.

**The feeling.** Start: the small irritation everyone knows, the belt that won't come out. The darkest moment: a
locked belt holding a person through a crash, the chest field going red. End: respect for a strap that makes two
decisions in a blink, and the urge to go and pull your own belt, slowly and then sharply.

**Written to PLAYBOOK §7.9 (the first film under it).** Stakes before mechanism in every chapter; the obvious fix
(a ratchet) shown failing; three spoken hero numbers (less than an inch, forty-five degrees, five adults) plus one
result (about three quarters); the catch shown as an event; no history as a citation; no classroom phrases; every
chapter ends on a click.

## Angles considered (3) → chosen
1. *Why does my seatbelt keep locking?* (the annoyance). The search's own question, and a warm door in, but it ends at
   "the child-seat mode". It becomes a payoff inside the film (C09), the moment viewers say "so *that's* why".
2. **The seatbelt that knows** (the mechanism mystery, then its cost). **Chosen as the spine:** the title's promise;
   the two weights are drawable and surprising; the catch (the lock is too good) turns it into a trade-off.
3. *The belt built to let go* (load limiters). The strongest twist and the open loop at 0:45; on its own too abstract
   for a first beat, so it becomes the catch and the truce.

## Cold opens considered (3) → chosen
1. **Phone vs wall.** One driver's shoulder and belt, in section (ILLUSTRATION). The hand reaches for a phone: the belt
   glides out. Cut: the same frame, the car stops dead, the belt locks, the body is held. The voice asks where the
   chip is. **Chosen:** it shows the paradox in two motions by 0:06, and sentence 1 confirms NO CHIP?.
2. *The yank test.* A real hand pulls a real belt slowly, then sharply (footage, if licensed). Strong and true, but
   the stakes (a crash) aren't in it; it moves to 0:10 as the proof that breaks the wrong model.
3. *Upside down.* A car on its roof, the occupant held. Rejected: it shows the answer's least important part first
   (the tilt rule) and leans on fear (§7.1).

## The five beats (PLAYBOOK §5.4)
1. **The paradox (0:00–0:45):** phone vs wall; the wrong model (the computer) and its break; the stakes; the promise
   in the title's words; the open loop (the belt that lets go).
2. **The trick (0:45–2:50):** the ratchet that can't tell slow from fast (the obvious fix, failing) → the heavy disc
   that falls behind (the belt sensor) → the prediction question (you haven't moved: what locks it?) → the ball that
   keeps going (the car sensor) → the rule: less than an inch → past forty-five degrees, gravity locks it.
3. **The payoff inside the trick (2:50–3:20):** "why does my seatbelt keep locking?": the child-seat mode.
4. **The catch (3:20–4:00):** a lock that is too good: the whole crash through a strap; older chests break.
5. **The truce (4:00–5:10):** the pretensioner (the one electronic part) pulls the slack in; the lock holds; the
   torsion bar twists and lets you go at a set force; Renault's six, then four kilonewtons, with an airbag; about
   three quarters fewer serious chest injuries; the cost: you travel further.
6. **The landing (5:10–5:35):** the viewer's own belt: pull it slowly, then sharply; one line on the trade-off; one
   forward line.

## Signature moments (2–3 to rewind and share)
1. **The disc that falls behind (1:35).** Macro section of the reel, true rotation: the reel and its loose heavy disc
   turn together on a slow pull; on the jerk the reel spins up and the disc visibly lags (a growing amber angle between
   two index marks), the little arm swings out and *clacks* into the ring of teeth. The HUD's REEL and DISC speeds split
   apart on the same driver as the picture.
2. **The ball keeps going (2:15).** The car stops; the steel ball in its cup does not: it rolls up the cone's wall and
   lifts the pawl. Prediction question first, a 2 s hold on the reveal. The HUD shows the car's deceleration crossing
   the 0.7 g line (silent, HUD only) and the belt payout stopping before the 25 mm mark.
3. **The bar that twists (4:25).** The torsion bar inside the reel, drawn as a steel rod: at the design force it starts
   to twist (FEA field yellow → red along its length), the reel turns *against* the locked teeth, the belt pays out a
   little; the chest field drops from red to amber. The HUD's BELT FORCE rises and then goes flat at its set limit.

## Visual world (inside the brand, §6)
- **The car as a living blueprint:** a side section of one front seat, the occupant as a neutral outline (no face, no
  identity), the belt as a cyan band from the pillar to the buckle; the camera dives from the cabin into the reel and
  back.
- **Colour = meaning:** cyan for the belt under load; amber for the decision points (the lagging disc, the ball, the
  pawl); crimson for the chest load field and the crash; emerald for the balance (the limiter's flat line, the belt
  holding at its set force).
- **Mass and sound (§7.9.9):** the reel's ratchet clack, the belt's nylon rasp, the hiss and thump of the pretensioner,
  the creak of the twisting bar. Nothing turns in empty space for long.
- **Real footage (§8.8):** a hand pulling a belt slowly then sharply; a belt winding in; a crash-test sled run if a
  commercial licence can be found (Pixabay API first). Never a render passed off as real.
- **Labels:** the reel, the disc, the ball and the bar are drawn after the patents, not a maker's drawing:
  ILLUSTRATION, and NOT TO SCALE where the ball and arm are enlarged.

## Risks and how the script handles them
- **"No chip"** is true of the lock (the patents' inertia sensors, C03–C04) but not of every part of a modern belt:
  the pretensioner is fired by the airbag's crash sensors (C10). The script says "the lock … doesn't need one" and later
  names the pretensioner as the electronic part. It never says no seatbelt has electronics.
- **Designs vary:** the film shows one belt sensor (a lagging disc) and one car sensor (a ball in a cup), each from a
  patent, and says "one common design" once.
- **Tall and heavy occupants:** said as a worry ("engineers worry"), never as a measured effect (C16 is a `claim`).
- **The tilt rule:** the standard's numbers only (15° must not lock, beyond 45° must lock); no claim about driveways.

## Cut v3: realism over diagrams (owner's review of cut v2, 2026-11-13)
The owner: "first moment itself feels lost interest; low level visuals; not at all near to reality; still image while
saying reaching phone and jerk; use ultra realistic animation for the science, otherwise mostly real clips". The story,
the voice and the timing stay; the picture is rebuilt (`remotion/edit.ts`, one shot list on the VO clock):
- **People only from real clips.** The opening is a real driver's hand going to the phone (Pixabay 133694), cut on
  "crashing" to a real NCAP frontal impact (NHTSA test 11664): the first ten seconds are all real. The catch, the
  airbag and the forward travel are NHTSA's own high-speed films (tests 11664 and 11500, a THOR dummy loading its belt).
  A procedural crash-test dummy was built and dropped: it read as a toy (first cabin test, 2026-11-14).
- **Objects in photoreal 3D (Cycles).** The belt at the pillar: woven webbing threaded through the D-ring, an autumn road
  outside (CC0 HDRI), the weave sliding by the model's own payout, so "slowly it lets you go, jerk it and it stops dead"
  is seen on the real object. The retractor in steel cutaway: frame and its ring of teeth, spool, the heavy disc, the
  arm, the ball in its cone, the pretensioner's charge, the torsion bar in a cut spool glowing in the stress colours.
- **Physics corrected in the 3D.** The car sensor's lever now catches teeth on the disc's rim (holding the disc, so the
  reel's next degrees are pure lag and the same arm locks it; P86, P98), not the frame's own teeth, which would lock
  nothing; the naive hook rides on the reel; the reel turns the right way for payout; the lever rests on the ball by
  construction (checked in `blender/retractor.py`).
- **One signature, made honest.** The jerk's lock takes about a fifth of a second; in the disc shot the simulation runs
  slowed so the arm catches on "The arm catches the teeth", and the picture says SLOW MOTION.
