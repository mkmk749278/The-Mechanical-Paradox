# Film 1: train wheels, brief (package first; PLAYBOOK §13)

**Slug** `train-wheels` · **Slate** blueprint Video 1 ("The Wheel Geometry Paradox") · **Length** about 5½ minutes
(narration 824 words; the topic set it, the blueprint's 4:15 was a guide)

## The viewer
Someone who has stood on a platform, heard a train screech on a bend, and never got a real answer. The engineering
student who has seen the 32-million-view "shape of train wheels" video and wants the next layer. They watch Practical
Engineering, Real Engineering, Steve Mould. US, UK, India in that order (autocomplete is nearly identical in all three).

## Search (research/sources.md, 2026-10-31, gl=US/GB/IN)
- **Main phrase:** *how do train wheels turn* (autocomplete in all three markets; "how do train wheels work" is broader).
- **Secondary:** why are train wheels conical · how do trains stay on the track · train wheel flange · why do train
  wheels make noise / train wheel flange squeal · why trains don't derail · coning of wheels in railway (IN).
- **The question people ask:** how does a solid axle go round a curve without one wheel slipping?
- **The gap:** the top 20 all stop at "the wheels are cones". None shows where the cone runs out (about a kilometer
  radius), what the screech really is (the tread sliding sideways, not the flange), or the cone's price (hunting). That
  is the film.

## Thumbnail concepts (2-element rule, squint test, ≤ 3 words; PLAYBOOK §13.3)
1. **Main — "NO STEERING":** a huge, lit cross-section of one coned wheel on its rail, the slope exaggerated and
   labelled NOT TO SCALE in small type off the subject; an amber pointer at the cone. Words: NO STEERING.
2. **Challenger — "0.7 mm":** the two wheels of an axle side by side, one ring visibly bigger, a dimension line between
   them in amber. Words: 0.7 mm. (The whole steering system in one number; the film pays it off at 1:20.)
3. **Challenger — the screech:** top view of a wheelset on a tight curve, the tread contact glowing crimson where it
   slides sideways. Words: THE REAL SCREECH. (Not "NOT THE FLANGE": the cause is widely accepted, not proven, claim
   C16, and a thumbnail can't carry a hedge.)

## The promise the first 30 seconds must keep
A solid axle, a longer outer rail, no slip on a gentle curve: how? And the film tells you it will also explain the
failure and the sway, and that the screech isn't what you think.

## Owner's decision (2026-10-31): the first line
The blueprint's fixed sentence ended "…without ever derailing"; all six test viewers flagged it as false (the film
itself shows a curve derailment). Owner chose: **"A one-hundred-ton locomotive has no steering wheel, no differential
gears, and a solid steel axle. Yet it rounds curves at high speed, and its wheels stay on the rails."** (claim C03)
