# Film 1: train wheels, treatment

**Logline.** A solid steel axle can't steer, yet it rounds every gentle curve without a slip. The trick is a cone so
shallow the steering lives in less than a millimeter, and the same cone that steers the train also tries to shake it
off the rails.

**The broken intuition (0:00–0:12).** Two wheels locked together must turn at the same speed; the outer rail is
longer; so one wheel must slip. It doesn't.

**The feeling.** Start: the mild itch of "wait, how?". Darkest moment: the cone runs out on a tight curve and the
steel starts sliding and screaming; then Nadal's limit, the wheel climbing. End: the quiet "oh" of a sound you've heard
all your life finally making sense.

## Angles considered (3) → chosen
1. *The cone explained* (what the 32M-view video does). Rejected: the viewer already has it.
2. *Where the cone fails*: the curve limit and the screech. Strong, but negative on its own.
3. **The cone's whole life: it steers (cheap, perfect), it runs out (tight curves, the screech, the flange climb),
   and it costs (hunting).** Chosen: every chapter turns the last one's answer into a new problem, and the ending is
   a real engineering trade-off nobody in the top 20 shows.

## Cold opens considered (3) → chosen
1. **The owner's fixed line** (blueprint §6.3): "A one-hundred-ton locomotive has no steering wheel…" Chosen: it is
   the owner's directive, and it states the paradox in 8 seconds. Picture: the wheelset alone, floating, spinning on
   its axle, then the track draws in under it as a curve.
2. The screech first: black screen, the sound of a tight curve, a waveform. Kept as the opening of chapter 3.
3. The two-point-four-meter rail: two rails race round a quarter turn and the outer one overshoots. Kept as the
   second beat of the cold open.

## Signature moments (the shots people rewind)
1. **The bank card (1:20).** A macro push into the two contact patches, the rolling radii drawn as live dimension
   lines; at full slide the difference reads 0.70 mm, and a bank card (a plain, unbranded card, to scale) slides in
   between the two dimension lines and is *thicker*. The whole steering system, thinner than a card.
2. **The rail race (0:12).** Top view: two contact points leave the start line of a quarter turn together; the inner
   one finishes first and a crimson ribbon of 2.36 m keeps unrolling on the outer rail. Then the cone version: both
   finish together.
3. **The wave drawn by the axle (3:15).** The wheelset seen from above, a long-exposure trace of its center drawing a
   perfect sine 17.2 m long on the blueprint grid, the Klingel formula building term by term beside it; then the
   speed rises past critical and the trace grows until the flanges strike, each strike a crimson flash on the stress
   field at the flange root.

## The visual world inside the brand
- The track is a blueprint drawing, never photo-real: rail heads as clean extruded profiles, sleepers as thin slate
  lines. The wheelset is the one solid, lit object: specular steel tread, cast-iron gray web.
- **Colour = meaning:** the cone and its rolling radii in **amber** (friction, the paradox point); the lateral slide
  and restoring tendency in **emerald** (equilibrium); creep and sliding contact in **crimson**; the flange contact
  force as a **crimson** stress bloom; on the curve map the "clean rolling" band is **emerald**, the "flange contact"
  band **crimson**. Cyan (compression) appears only as the wheel load pressing down in the Nadal beat.
- Camera personality: patient and precise, like a measuring instrument. Slow orbits round the wheelset; macro pushes
  to the contact patch whenever the narration goes down a scale.
- **Honesty labels:** the cone slope is exaggerated wherever it must be seen → NOT TO SCALE. HUD values from the
  textbook wheelset → MODEL (sources C10, C13, C14, C21). The locomotive is generic, unbranded.

## HUD (top-right, ticking with the VO)
`CURVE R`, `SHIFT y`, `Δr`, `FLANGE GAP`, later `λ (KLINGEL)`, `f`, `SPEED`. Every value from model.py or claims.

## Sound palette
Low sub drone; relay clicks as the blueprint assembles; quartz ticks on the HUD; a filtered pink-noise roll for steel
on steel; the squeal synthesized as a tonal ring (curve squeal is "a highly disturbing tonal sound", claim C16 source) only in chapter 3, kept below a painful level; a 45 Hz
thud for the flange strike. Music drops out 1.5 s before "That is the entire steering system".
