# Panel summary

## Top 7 problems, ranked

**1. The quiz gives away its own answer.** All five of us drifted here. The hook already says "it is built to slip," so asking whether it should grab hard feels like a test you've been handed the answers to. Marcus also found "Take a second" condescending.
- Current line: "So here is a question for you. Should a brake like this grab as hard as it can, and stop you dead? Take a second. Most of us would say yes."
- Rewrite: "But why not just grab as hard as it can, and stop you dead? That sounds safest. It isn't."

**2. The fake ending before the twist.** Liam nearly left here, and Helen thought it was the end.
- Current line: "That is the deal. The wedge, on its own, would stop you far too hard. The spring sets the limit. Together, they turn a grab into a slide. But all of this only works one way. Down."
- Rewrite: "The wedge grabs. The spring sets the limit. Together, they turn a grab into a slide. But there's a catch. All of this only works one way. Down."

**3. Paragraph 2 reads like an agenda.** It drags at 1.25x and spends the energy the hook just built.
- Current line: "So how does a falling box brake itself? Why would anyone build a brake that slips? And what about the danger it was never built to stop, an elevator that runs away upward?"
- Rewrite: "So how does a falling box brake itself, and why on purpose let it slip? And stay for the danger this brake can't stop at all."

**4. "The work of the springs" is misleading physics.** Katrin and Marcus both flagged it: springs store energy, they don't absorb it.
- Current line: "One maker's manual tells you the braking comes from metal cutting into the rail surface, from friction, and from the work of the springs."
- Rewrite: "One maker's manual says the braking comes from friction, and from metal being cut away from the rail surface."

**5. "Fixed friction" is too absolute.** Katrin's concern: friction changes with heat and speed, and the load matters too.
- Current line: "A fixed squeeze means a fixed friction. And a fixed friction means steady slowing."
- Rewrite: "A steady squeeze means steady friction, and that means fairly steady slowing."

**6. "Safety" and "safety gear" are never defined, and "rated at" is jargon.** Helen and Liam got lost on these.
- "The first famous safety did exactly that." becomes "The first famous safety brake did exactly that."
- "…but only on elevators rated at sixty-three centimeters a second or less." becomes "…but only on slow elevators, built to run at sixty-three centimeters a second or less."
- "And the classic safety gear? It could not help, because it only grips going down." becomes "And our wedge brake? It can't help. It only grips going down."

**7. The upward fix is thin, and its source is confusing.**
- Current line: "One United States mine safety study counted at least eighteen elevators…"
- Rewrite: "One United States safety study counted at least eighteen elevators that hit the top of their shafts in just five years." If those were mine elevators, say so plainly instead.
- Current line: "It can be a brake that clamps the ropes, or safety gear built to grip both ways."
- Rewrite: "It can be a clamp that squeezes the ropes themselves, or wedges built to grip in both directions."
- Bonus fix for the three technical words in one breath: "…must not be set off by anything electric, hydraulic or pneumatic" becomes "…says nothing electric, and no fluid or air pressure, may set it off."

## Rewritten first 12 seconds

> "The ropes holding your elevator snap. You fall for a fraction of a second, then stop within about a meter. No motor saved you. No computer. The fall itself pulled the brake. And that brake is built to slip."

## The single change that would most raise satisfaction

Cut the spoiled quiz, and turn "That is the deal" into a fast "But there's a catch" pivot. Right now the middle has one dead beat and the best twist arrives sounding like an afterthought. Fixing those two seams keeps all five of us leaning in from start to finish.

## Verdict

Not quite ready. The mechanism and the physics are strong and trustworthy. Fix the quiz, the false ending and the two physics wordings (problems 1, 2, 4 and 5) and it's ready.
