## Standards
- **[EN81] EN 81-20:2020 as published in AS 1735.1.2:2021** (Standards Australia preview pages 88–92),
  https://www.normsplash.com/Samples/AS/183538815/AS-1735.1.2-2021-en-2.pdf
  - 5.6.2.1.1.1 "The safety gear shall be capable of operating in the downward direction and capable of stopping a car
    carrying the rated load … at the tripping speed of the overspeed governor, or if the suspension devices break, by
    gripping the guide rails, and of holding the car … there."
  - 5.6.2.1.2.1 "Car safety gear: a) shall be of the progressive type, or b) may be of the instantaneous type if the
    rated speed of the lift does not exceed 0,63 m/s."
  - 5.6.2.1.3 "For progressive safety gear the average retardation in the case of free fall of the car with rated load
    … shall lie between 0,2 gn and 1 gn."
  - 5.6.2.1.4.1 "The release and automatic reset of a safety gear on the car … shall only be possible by raising the
    car"; 5.6.2.1.4.3 "After the release of the safety gear it shall require the intervention of a competent
    maintenance person to return the lift to service."
  - 5.6.2.1.6.4 "Safety gears shall not be tripped by devices, which operate electrically, hydraulically or
    pneumatically."
  - 5.6.2.2.1.1 a) "tripping of the overspeed governor for the safety gear shall occur at a speed at least equal to
    115 % of the rated speed and less than … 4) 1,25 ⋅ v + 0,25/v … for progressive safety gear for rated speeds
    exceeding 1,0 m/s."
  - 5.6.2.2.1.2 "the maximum distance between tripping points on the governor shall not exceed 250 mm related to the
    movement of the governor rope."
  - 5.6.2.2.1.3 d) "the overspeed governor rope shall be tensioned by a pulley with a tensioning weight."
  - Table 11: "Excessive speed in up direction (traction lifts only) — Ascending car overspeed protection means (5.6.6)".
- **[SEA] Seattle Building Code 2018, 3011.6.12** (ASME A17.1-derived), https://up.codes/s/suspension-systems-and-their-connections
  - 3011.6.12.4 "The minimum number of hoisting ropes used is three for traction elevators and two for drum-type
    elevators." 3011.6.12.3 factor of safety table (e.g. 7.60 at 50 fpm to 11.90 at 1400–2000 fpm, passenger).

## Manufacturers and patents
- **[BODE] Bode overspeed governor manual (2024)**, https://bode-components.com/wp-content/uploads/2024-08-12__BMA_OG_V2024-2-EN_10100197.pdf
  - "In this case, the pendulum blocks the overspeed governor wheel. It is mechanically blocked and cannot rotate
    anymore. The lift car continues moving in the shaft, the overspeed governor rope, guided in the wedge groove,
    blocks and engages the safety gear of the lift. The car will stop."
- **[OTIS-GOV] US 8,931,598 B2, Otis Elevator Co. (filed 2007, published 2015)**, https://patents.google.com/patent/US8931598B2/en
  - The rope loop "is also connected to the elevator car 12, ensuring that the angular velocity of the tripping sheave 18
    is related to the speed of the elevator car 12"; rotation gives "a centrifugal force acting to propel the masses
    away from the sheave axis of rotation"; "The coupler commonly includes a spring connected between the sheave and
    one of the masses."
- **[OTIS-WEDGE] US 10,407,278 B2, Otis Elevator Co. (2019)**, https://patents.google.com/patent/US10407278B2/en
  - "the frictional force further pulls the wedge to move upward"; prior art: "a downward acting force is exerted on the
    counter wedge through an elastic force of multiple disc springs"; test: "an acceleration value is substantially
    stabilized at approximately 0.9 g".
- **[KONE] US 6,109,398 A, KONE Oy (2000)**, https://patents.google.com/patent/US6109398A/en
  - "the compression applied by the safety gear to the guide rail is set by means of a spring"; "the magnitude of the
    compressive force determines the deceleration of the elevator".
- **[WITTUR] Wittur safety devices page**, https://www.wittur.com/en/elevator-components/mechanical-packages/safety-devices.aspx
  - CSGB "progressive type safety gears": "a combination of disc springs and braking elements always in pairs",
    "activated by a movable tripping wedge", "The braking force is adjustable by the disc spring assembly."
- **[BF] SLC progressive safety gear BF_D-1 manual (2019)**, https://slc-liftco.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/BF-Typ1_manual_en_0219.pdf
  - "The braking effect is caused by metal cutting in the rail surface, friction and spring tensioning work."
  - "The rubbed-off particles have to be removed and the braking marks have to be grinded down."
  - Variants "Braking downwards BF1D-1 · Braking up- and downwards BF2D-1 · Braking upwards BF3D-1".
- **[PFB] PFB srl, "Lift safety gear: what it is, how it works"**, https://pfb.it/en/news/325810/lift-safety-gear-how-it-works
  - Instantaneous gear "brings the installation to an abrupt and immediate stop", "rated speeds up to 0.63 m/s";
    progressive gear "applies a gradual braking action".

## The upward danger
- **[MSHA] T. D. Barkand, "Elevator safety: give the miner a brake", US Mine Safety and Health Administration**,
  https://arlweb.msha.gov/s&hinfo/techrpt/hoist/paper4.htm
  - counterweight "equal to the weight of the car containing about 45% of its rated load"; safeties "do not provide
    protection in the upward direction"; "at least 18 documented cases of ascending elevators striking the overhead"
    over "a five-year period" (US, counterweighted mine elevators); CSA-B44 ascending car overspeed rule "went into
    effect April 1, 1990"; rope brake as a device against overspeed up and down.
- **[TKE] TK Elevator, Classroom on Demand: Counterweights**, https://www.tkelevator.com/us-en/tools/classroom-on-demand/counterweights.html
  — counterweight = cab weight plus 40–50 % of rated load (as summarised in search; second source for the balance).
